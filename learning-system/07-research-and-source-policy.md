# Research & Source Quality Policy

## Source Hierarchy
Choose according to the claim:
1. Official standards/specifications from the responsible body.
2. Official software documentation and technical manuals.
3. Government scientific agencies and authoritative data providers.
4. University textbooks/open textbooks and recognized academic material.
5. Peer-reviewed papers and systematic reviews.
6. Professional organizations and reputable training materials.
7. High-quality practitioner articles for context.
8. Forums/social media only as troubleshooting clues or labeled user experience.

## Verify Each Important Source
Record exact title, author/organization, publication/version date, URL, access date when freshness matters, relevant section, supported claim, limitations/prerequisites, and whether current/archived/draft/superseded.

## Standards & Field Work
- Verify the current edition with the issuing organization.
- Distinguish published standards from drafts.
- Read project specifications; a standard may not define every project requirement.
- Do not claim compliance without actual evidence.
- Use qualified supervision for instrument operation, field safety, and sign-off.
- Check AI-generated field instructions against manuals and approved procedures.

## Software Documentation
Use docs matching the selected/installed version. Avoid incompatible examples. Record version-sensitive behavior. Run tests or minimal reproductions when possible.

## Common AI Research Failures
Fabricated citations; correct title but wrong edition; draft described as final; snippets treated as full reading; deprecated APIs; unsupported transfer between countries/projects; correlation mistaken for causation; plausible map mistaken for valid analysis.

## Citation Rule
Cite material technical claims that are not basic reasoning or directly demonstrated. Prefer exact sections over generic homepages.

## Review Timing
Recheck fast-changing software docs, APIs, AI tools, and standards when beginning the relevant unit and before relying on them in a deliverable. Stable math references need not be rechecked every session.
